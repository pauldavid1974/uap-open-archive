# Robertson Panel

**Label: official.** [CIA-UAP-002](../records/cia-uap-002.md) is the catalog entry for the Scientific Advisory Panel on Unidentified Flying Objects, convened by the CIA's Office of Scientific Intelligence, with correspondence and reports dated 1952–1953.

The catalog description says the panel's primary conclusion was that "flying saucers" did not pose a direct physical threat to the United States, and that it found no evidence they were hostile foreign artifacts or a reason to revise scientific concepts. It says the panel did see an indirect threat: public fascination, fed by a sensationalist press, could clog intelligence channels, and a "morbid national psychology" could be exploited. The panel recommended a policy of "debunking" to "strip the UFO subject of its mystery," plus training so military personnel would recognize misidentified objects.

A later CIA memo, [CIA-UAP-D021](../records/cia-uap-d021.md), cites a finding by Dr. Howard Robertson of that panel that "almost all the sightings represented no threat to the U.S."

**Label: analysis.** The catalog blurb for CIA-UAP-002 does not mention the Tremonton film or seagulls. Secondary histories say the panel viewed that film and favored birds. This archive did not open the PDF, so it does not confirm that detail. The Tremonton case file records a later Air Force "likely seabirds" assessment separately.
