# Gulf of Oman "cold orbs," September 8, 2021

**Status: unexplained.** The crew report describes repeated objects. The video that was released is a phone pointed at a screen, and the aircraft's own recorder was not working. The objects are not identified.

## In plain language

During a nighttime live-fire training flight on September 8, 2021, the crew of an Air Force AC-130J gunship over the Gulf of Oman reported many small, cold-looking spots on their infrared screen, close to the water, some of them moving fast and seeming to react when the aircraft used its weapons. They said they could not record with the plane's video recorder. The six clips released with the report are phone videos of that screen.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.** [DOW-UAP-D101](../records/dow-uap-d101.md) is an Intelligence Information Report. The catalog says an IIR is unevaluated reporting, not a finished assessment. The crew first saw two objects after dropping a water-activated flare. The objects are described as stationary, about four feet across, 0 to 20 feet above the water. Across the sortie the crew reported about 25 instances of similar "cold orbs," with visual speed estimates from 250 to 1,300 miles per hour, "maneuvering aggressively," flying in formations, and appearing to react to weapons. The crew said the digital video recorder was not functioning. The catalog says those characterizations are the reporter's subjective interpretation and should not be read as a conclusive finding.

**Label: official.** [DOW-UAP-PR117](../records/dow-uap-pr117.md) through PR122 are the clips. PR117's description says the footage is a secondary capture: a cellphone filming the infrared display. It is not the original sensor recording. Filming a screen can blur or flicker. The description of the picture itself is only that an area of contrast stays generally in the center for 16 seconds. The same description says the six clips were captured at the time, and points back to D101.

## Strongest and weakest points

**Label: analysis.**

- Strongest: a trained crew, many reported instances in one flight, and an infrared system, described in a standard intelligence report.
- Weakest: speeds are the crew's visual estimates. "Cold" on an infrared screen can be a balloon, a bird, spray, or a glitch. The only video is a phone filming a monitor, and the report says the real recorder was broken. This archive did not watch the clips. File sizes from DVIDS are on each record page.
