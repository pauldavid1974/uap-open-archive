# Apollo 17 "three dots," December 1972

**Status: unexplained.** The Department of War's own preliminary note says the dots might be a physical object in the scene, and that a full analysis was still to come. That follow-up is not in the catalog this archive is built from.

## In plain language

One Apollo 17 photo shows three dots in a triangle in the lunar sky, visible when the picture is enlarged. The government says it has the original film and that NASA and the Department of War will release a full analysis when it is done. As of the September 29, 2026 catalog, and as of the October 4, 2026 archived PURSUE homepage, that analysis is not listed.

The other Apollo 17 files in this release are crew transcripts and debriefs. They are full of talk about bright particles, ice, paint, and panels from the spacecraft. They are context. They are not, by their titles, an analysis of the three dots.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.** [NASA-UAP-VM006](../records/nasa-uap-vm006.md): the image contains three "dots" in a triangular formation in the lower right of the lunar sky. There is no consensus. "New preliminary US government analysis suggests the image feature is potentially the result of a physical object in the scene." The government obtained the original film. "The results of the full NASA and DOW analysis will be released when completed."

A physical object in the scene can mean nearby debris, ice, or a panel. The sentence does not say the object is a craft. This archive did not open the PURSUE image file.

## Which NASA frame it is

**Label: official** for the NASA and Lunar and Planetary Institute pages. **Label: analysis** for the match to the PURSUE file.

The catalog row does not print a NASA frame number. The frame is AS17-147-22470.

- NASA's photo library lists that id and offers a 4400 by 4600 pixel scan: <https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/SearchPhotos/photo.pl?mission=AS17&roll=147&frame=22470> (retrieved October 6, 2026).
- The Lunar and Planetary Institute's Apollo Image Atlas, in a copy archived August 21, 2022, describes the same frame as a 70 mm Hasselblad, magazine 147/A, 60 mm lens, sun elevation 13 degrees, film SO-368, color, taken before the first moonwalk ("PRE EVA 1"), and captioned as a lunar-module window pan: <https://web.archive.org/web/20220821114518/https://www.lpi.usra.edu/resources/apollo/frame/?AS17-147-22470>. The live LPI page returned HTTP 403 to this project on October 6, 2026.

An independent fact-check on October 5, 2026 compared the PURSUE image (`war.gov/medialink/ufo/release_1/nasa-uap-vm6-apollo-17-1972.jpg`, via an archived copy) with NASA's 4400 by 4600 scan and reported that they are the same scan, with a yellow callout added on the PURSUE copy, and that the three dots sit in the same place. This archive did not repeat that pixel comparison. The frame number is verified by the NASA pages plus that comparison. It is still not a number the catalog prints.

The same fact-check noted that image alt text on the PURSUE page mentions astronaut shadows, while the LPI description of this frame is a window pan before the crew went outside. This archive did not re-read that alt text.

## Strongest and weakest points

**Label: analysis.**

- Strongest: the government took the photo seriously enough to pull the original film and promise a result.
- Weakest: the promise is unfulfilled in the sources this archive could check. Crew transcripts from the same mission describe particles and drifting panels, which are ordinary candidates. Lens reflections also make geometric dots. None of that is proved here.

## Gap

**Label: analysis.** The promised NASA/DOW analysis is an open item. See [open questions](../gaps/open-questions.md).
