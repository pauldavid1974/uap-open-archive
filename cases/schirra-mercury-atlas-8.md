# Wally Schirra's Mercury-Atlas 8 audio, October 3, 1962

**Status: disputed.** The dispute is not inside the catalog. The catalog describes particles coming off the capsule, and a burst of light whose source he could not identify. A separate public story says Schirra saw a "tag-along" craft. That word is not in the catalog description. This archive did not listen to the audio file.

## In plain language

On October 3, 1962, astronaut Wally Schirra, flying Sigma 7, talked about little white objects that seemed to come from his own capsule. He compared them to particles and to metal shavings. He also described a burst of light in the window. NASA posted an excerpt of that audio in PURSUE Release 02.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.** [NASA-UAP-D012](../records/nasa-uap-d012.md). The catalog puts three of his phrases in quotation marks: "little white objects that tend to come from the capsule itself and drift off," "particles," and "lathe shavings." The rest of the description is the catalog's own sentences, not marked as a quotation: he describes a burst of light in the window, whose source he cannot identify, and he speculates that the observation corresponds with the moment the sun passes below the horizon during sunset. Do not shorten that into a quotation he did not speak.

The description does not use the word "tag-along." It does not mention a nuclear test.

The same particles are part of the longer Mercury "fireflies" story. See [astronaut particles](astronaut-particles.md). On Mercury-Atlas 9, the catalog says NASA later decided John Glenn's "fireflies" were frozen condensation. That sentence is about Glenn's fireflies, in [NASA-UAP-D010](../records/nasa-uap-d010.md). It is not a sentence about Schirra's light burst.

## Claims this archive did not re-check

**Label: advocate.** Public interviews with Harald Malmgren in 2024 have been summarized, in UFO press, as a claim that classified footage showed a tag-along object, that Schirra saw something similar, and that this was tied to the 1962 Bluegill Triple Prime nuclear test. This build did not watch those interviews. A pointer, not a finding: discussions of the claim circulate under his name. Treat them as claims.

**Label: skeptic.** Researcher Douglas Dean Johnson has published archival arguments that the falling object in Bluegill Triple Prime footage is the rocket booster, and that Malmgren's 1960s access was not what the interviews implied. This build did not re-read those articles. They are the skeptical side of the argument, not a fact established here. One of his essays is at <https://douglasjohnson.ghost.io/bluegill-triple-prime-nuclear-ufo/> and another is at <https://douglasjohnson.ghost.io/harald-malmgren-history-vs-fantasy/>.

**Label: analysis.** Nothing in the PURSUE description supports a tag-along narrative. Nothing in it proves a negative either, because the audio was not played for this archive. DVIDS hosts the excerpt as video id 1007877: <https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007877/nasa-uap-d012-mercury-atlas-8-audio-excerpt-october-3-1962>.
