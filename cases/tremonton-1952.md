# Tremonton, Utah film, July 2, 1952

**Status: disputed.** Navy photo analysts and a later Air Force assessment do not say the same thing. The film in this release is a surviving print, and part of it is something else entirely.

## In plain language

On July 2, 1952, Navy photographer Delbert C. Newhouse filmed bright objects in the sky near Tremonton, Utah. The Air Force's Project Blue Book kept a case file. A Navy photo lab looked at this film and a 1950 Montana film and said the objects did not look like ordinary phenomena or known aircraft, while warning that its own conclusion was presumptive. Years later, an Air Force file in this same release says the Tremonton objects were likely seabirds. Both sentences are in the official catalog. They are not reconciled here.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.**

- [DOW-UAP-D102](../records/dow-uap-d102.md): Blue Book file. The catalog calls Newhouse "a U.S. Navy Warrant Officer specializing in photography," with more than 1,000 hours of aerial photography, and says he was assessed as honest and reliable. He reported 12 to 14 reflective objects and filmed them with a handheld Bell and Howell camera. A different row, [DOW-UAP-PR159](../records/dow-uap-pr159.md), calls him a chief warrant officer. Both phrases are in the catalog. See [his entity page](../entities/delbert-newhouse.md).
- [DOW-UAP-PR159](../records/dow-uap-pr159.md): a digitization of a 16mm Kodachrome print. From the 55-second mark the film switches to unrelated footage of what looks like a sewing-equipment demonstration. The catalog says the surviving footage may be a composite or reused print, and that mountain scenery from Idaho that originally accompanied the film is not in this digitization. The DVIDS page lists the length as 1 minute 10 seconds. The file is about 76 MB. It was not downloaded into this repository.
- [DOW-UAP-D098](../records/dow-uap-d098.md): the Naval Photographic Interpretation Center's memorandum on the Montana 1950 film and the Utah 1952 film. The Center assessed that the objects showed characteristics inconsistent with natural phenomena or commonly known aerospace technologies. The catalog calls the conclusions tentative. (The catalog says "Montana," not "Great Falls." Great Falls is a name used in later writing. It is not in this description.)
- [DOW-UAP-D103](../records/dow-uap-d103.md): a check of whether the objects were "pillow balloons." General Mills, then the only known maker, was contacted in December 1952. The group's consensus was that the objects resembled pillow balloons. One General Mills representative was less convinced, saying they looked too bright and seemed to maneuver too fast. No matching balloon release is described in the portion of the catalog text read for this page.
- [DOW-UAP-D104](../records/dow-uap-d104.md): Newhouse's Navy personnel record, cited by Blue Book as the basis for calling him an expert photographer.
- [DOW-UAP-D105](../records/dow-uap-d105.md): an Air Force "flying discs" file for September–December 1952. The catalog says pages 26–36 refer to the Tremonton incident and that the Air Force later assessed the objects were likely seabirds.

## What this page does not claim

**Label: analysis.** Secondary histories say the 1953 Robertson Panel watched a gull film and favored birds. The catalog description of the panel's report, [CIA-UAP-002](../records/cia-uap-002.md), does not mention Tremonton or gulls. It does say the panel recommended "debunking" to "strip the UFO subject of its mystery." This archive did not open that PDF, so it does not confirm or deny a Tremonton conclusion inside it.

The Condon Report's view of this film is not in the PURSUE catalog description searched for this page. It is not restated here as fact.

## Strongest and weakest points

**Label: analysis.**

- Strongest: an experienced Navy photographer, a film that still exists in some form, and a Navy lab memo that did not accept an ordinary explanation at the time.
- Weakest: the released print is incomplete and, after 55 seconds, is not the sighting. A later official assessment says seabirds. Balloon specialists mostly agreed with balloons, with one dissent. Without the original camera, the range, and a frame-by-frame study, this repository cannot pick a winner. The PDFs and the film were not opened here.
