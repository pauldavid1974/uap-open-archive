# Puerto Rico radar case, November 19, 1964

**Status: disputed.** The memos describe a visual sighting and a very fast radar track. They do not identify the object. The spreadsheet also disagrees with itself about the date.

## In plain language

A Navy pilot reported a delta-shaped object, about the size of a jet fighter, near Puerto Rico. Radar on the destroyer USS Gyatt tracked a contact at about 3,800 knots, which the catalog converts as about 4,373 miles per hour. CIA analysts ruled out the U.S. YF-12A / OXCART spy plane and wrote that foreign reconnaissance of the Atlantic Fleet Weapons Range was conceivable. That is a hypothesis in the memo, not a solved case.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.** [CIA-UAP-D022](../records/cia-uap-d022.md): memoranda between the CIA's Assistant Director for Special Activities and the Assistant Director for Scientific Intelligence. They refer to an incident on November 19, 1964. A naval aviator described a "delta-shaped" object "about the size of a jet fighter" and visually estimated Mach 3 (about 2,200 mph). An AN/SPS-49 radar on USS Gyatt measured 3,800 knots (about 4,373 mph). In the opinion of the AD/SI, the object was conceivably tied to foreign intelligence gathering, because missile testing in the Atlantic Fleet Weapons Range would be an attractive target. The AD/SA and AD/SI ruled out domestic experimental aircraft such as the YF-12A OXCART.

**Label: official.** [CIA-UAP-D023](../records/cia-uap-d023.md): briefing notes for Walter N. Elder, executive assistant to the Director of Central Intelligence. It repeats the November 19, 1964 date, says the Gyatt radar contact tracked at more than 3,800 knots for 21 minutes, and says an F-8 Crusader pilot added visual estimates.

## The date problem

**Label: analysis.** The description says November 19, 1964. The title of D022 says 1965. The incident-date cell says 2/1/65. The location cell says Caribbean Sea, not "Puerto Rico." Both wordings are left as the catalog wrote them. This archive did not open the PDF to see which date the memo itself uses as the event date. November 19, 1964 is the date the description states for the incident.

## Strongest and weakest points

**Label: analysis.**

- Strongest: a pilot's eyes and a ship's radar are both described, with a duration (21 minutes) in the briefing notes.
- Weakest: 1964 radar can produce false tracks. The memo itself treats foreign reconnaissance as conceivable, which would make this a secret aircraft, not something beyond aviation. No plot, no recording, and no range file is included. The PDF was not opened here.
