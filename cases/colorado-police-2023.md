# Colorado police phone videos, October 2023

**Status: unexplained.** A police officer filmed a silent, multicolored light. AARO blurred the bottom of the frames, removed the audio, and warned that a phone camera can distort shape and motion. The place, direction, and clock time are not in the catalog, so nobody outside can check what was in the sky.

This is one officer and one set of clips. It is not the 2019–2020 Colorado and Nebraska drone reports. Those are not in the PURSUE catalog. See [that case](colorado-nebraska-drones-2019-2020.md).

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.**

- [LLE-UAP-PR001](../records/lle-uap-pr001.md): 1 minute 19 seconds, October 2023, rear phone camera. The officer described the phenomenon as "silent" with "red, blue, and green flashing lights." AARO blurred the bottom half. From 0:48 to 1:19 the camera zooms in and out while tracking a light. AARO's comment: phone autofocus and automatic processing "may distort the apparent morphological and kinematic characteristics of distant subjects, particularly at high magnification."
- [LLE-UAP-PR002](../records/lle-uap-pr002.md): 15 minutes. The camera stays on an apparent light source the whole time. Same blur and the same autofocus warning.
- PR003 and PR004 are the other two videos (the catalog descriptions give their lengths on the record pages). D001–D004 are transcripts. AARO removed the audio. The transcripts are redacted to protect the officer's identity.
- [LLE-UAP-PR004](../records/lle-uap-pr004.md): the title says January 2024. The incident-date cell says October, 2023. Both are printed. This archive did not pick one.

DVIDS lists PR001 at about 7.4 MB and date taken 10.01.2023, location "COLORADO, US." That date-taken field is from the DVIDS page retrieved October 6, 2026. It is more specific than the catalog's "October, 2023," and it is still only a state.

## What a newspaper reported from the transcript

**Label: press.** Spencer McKee, Denver Gazette / Out There Colorado, September 22, 2026: <https://www.denvergazette.com/outtherecolorado/2026/09/22/feds-share-video-of-uap-that-was-captured-by-law-enforcement-in-colorado/>. This archive read the article on October 6, 2026. It is a news summary of the Department's posting, not a second measurement.

The article says the Department described an officer who saw the object on October 1, 2023, and that four clips were posted. It says a brief transcript has the officer describing an orb-like object about 300 feet up, a radar check that showed no aircraft, and an object that stayed still for about 15 minutes and then moved closer. It says the location in that transcript is redacted, and that the bottom of the video is blurred. On September 22 the catalog snapshots stored here contain only LLE-UAP-D001 of the four transcripts, which matches the article's "brief transcript."

The 300 feet, the radar check, and the 15 minutes are the Gazette's account of the officer's words in that transcript. They are not fields in the catalog spreadsheet. This archive did not open the transcript, so it does not requote them from the government file. LLE-UAP-D004's title says January 2024. The Gazette article is about the October 2023 report.

## Strongest and weakest points

**Label: analysis.**

- Strongest: a law-enforcement witness, several clips including a 15-minute one, and a government office that published them instead of ignoring them.
- Weakest: a silent multicolored light held in frame for a long time is also what a bright star or planet does near the horizon, and the phone warning says the image may lie about shape and motion. Because the location, direction, and time of night were removed, that idea cannot be tested from the public file. The Gazette's height and radar lines are the officer's transcribed account, as the newspaper reports it. They are not an independent radar plot in the catalog.
