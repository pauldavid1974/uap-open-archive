# AAWSAP contract papers and DIRDs, Release 06

**Status: disputed.** The word here means the argument about what this program really was. The 44 files in this release document a Defense Intelligence Agency contract for far-term aerospace research. They do not, in the catalog descriptions, settle claims about a hidden UFO investigation. Those claims are not re-litigated from books this archive did not read.

## In plain language

In 2008 the Defense Intelligence Agency started a contract program called the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program, AAWSAP. The contractor was Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS). PURSUE Release 06 posted the statement of objectives, the award, five contract changes, and 37 technical papers called DIRDs. Each DIRD's catalog description says it is one of 38. One of the 38 is not in this spreadsheet.

The papers are literature reviews of exotic ideas (warp metrics, antigravity concepts, lasers, materials). The catalog repeatedly says they should not be read as proof that the ideas work.

## What the official catalog says

**Label: official.**

- [DOW-UAP-D110](../records/dow-uap-d110.md), July 18, 2008 statement of objectives. AAWSAP is described as a DIA program active from 2008 to 2012, aimed at far-term foreign aerospace threats and breakthrough technologies out to 2050, not a straight-line forecast from then-current aircraft. Twelve areas are named, including lift, propulsion, control, power generation, "spatial or temporal translation," materials, configuration and structure, signature reduction, human interface, human effects, and armament. It asks for a final integrated threat assessment.
- [DOW-UAP-D111](../records/dow-uap-d111.md), September 22, 2008. The award instrument for work by Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies, Inc., turning the objectives into contract line items.
- [DOW-UAP-D112](../records/dow-uap-d112.md) through [D116](../records/dow-uap-d116.md): five modifications, P00001 through P00005, signed from September 2009 through September 2010. [D116](../records/dow-uap-d116.md) extends performance of contract HHM402-08-C-0072 to December 21, 2010, and says no additional funding was required.
- D117 through D153: 37 DIRDs. Every one of those descriptions says the paper is "one of 38 DIRDs" produced under AAWSAP between 2009 and 2011, and that the summary "should not be read as implying current validation."

Examples of the catalog's own caution, **label: official**:

- [D138](../records/dow-uap-d138.md), the warp-drive paper, "offers no viable engineering pathway toward a functioning propulsion system."
- [D135](../records/dow-uap-d135.md), the antigravity survey, says practical barriers are currently insurmountable, and that implying those ideas might yield propulsion "deviates significantly from mainstream physics consensus."
- [D128](../records/dow-uap-d128.md), on reported injuries near anomalous fields, "rely heavily on case comparisons lacking physical examinations."

All 44 AAWSAP rows are flagged redacted in the spreadsheet. The flag does not say how much was removed.

## What is not in these descriptions

**Label: analysis.** A text search of the AAWSAP titles and descriptions in this catalog does not turn up the program nickname AATIP, a Utah ranch, or a final integrated assessment as a delivered file. If those records exist, they are not these 44 rows. This project could not open aaro.mil (HTTP 403 on October 6, 2026), so it did not re-read AARO's 2024 historical report and does not quote that report.

## What was already public

**Label: analysis.** Do not call this contract trail a first release.

The July 2008 statement of objectives, the original contract order, and modification P00001 were already public before Release 06, through DIA FOIA releases collected by The Black Vault. The fact-check also notes that the solicitation text circulated as early as 2018, and it gives the solicitation number as HHM402-08-R-0211. That solicitation number is not in the catalog descriptions this page quotes. The contract number HHM402-08-C-0072 is in the catalog, on the later modifications. PURSUE gathers these papers with the DIRDs. Whether modifications P00002 through P00005 had been public before is **unconfirmed**. They were not found in that FOIA collection. Other copies are not ruled out. See [unverified claims](../gaps/unverified-claims.md).

- <https://www.theblackvault.com/documentarchive/the-advanced-aerospace-weapon-system-applications-program-aawsap-documentation/>
- <https://www.theblackvault.com/documentarchive/advanced-aerospace-weapon-systems-applications-program-aawsap-original-bid-solicitation/>

This archive did not re-download those PDFs on October 6, 2026. The "already public" conclusion is the October 5 fact-check's reading of those postings, adopted here because it corrects an earlier memo that called the whole trail new.

The catalog says there were 38 DIRDs and this release has 37 files with DIRD in the title (D117–D153). The missing paper is not named in those catalog descriptions. The same fact-check, citing the DIA's list of 38 titles (FOIA) and The Black Vault's copy of the 37 papers released March 25, 2022, identifies the withheld paper as a classified study of high-energy laser weapons. This archive did not re-open that DIA list, so the laser title is carried as that check's reading of the FOIA file, not as a sentence copied from it here. A pointer: <https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/dia/dia-aatip-reports.pdf>.

Dollar amounts are not in the catalog descriptions read for this page. A figure of about $22 million, and a line that BAASS was the only bidder, appear in secondary accounts of a 2009 DIA memo. This archive did not open that memo. The dollar figure is not restated here as a checked quotation. The memo as posted by The Black Vault: <https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/dia/AAWSAP/DR_Reid_Mtg_May09.pdf>.
